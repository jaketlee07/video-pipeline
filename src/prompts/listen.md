The words that differ are «marked», with a few words either side, and the last line of each spells out the difference as spoken words (numbers written out, punctuation dropped): judge that.

Most differences are not problems: the transcriber wrote the same spoken words its own way. That includes a name spelled differently but said the same ("Kimble" for "Kimball"), a number written another way ("1%" or "one percent" for "1 percent", "twenty sixteen" for "2016"), a contraction ("it's" for "it is" is NOT the same, but "its" for "it's" is), a homophone, punctuation, or a word split or joined ("Walmart's" for "Walmart s"). Call those "fine".

Call it "misread" when the narrator most likely said something different from the script: a wrong number or amount, a different word, a word or phrase skipped or added, a number or abbreviation read out wrong (a road number, a unit, a symbol, "St." or "U.S."), or a garbled or repeated stretch. Call it "unsure" when you can't tell which, such as a name the transcriber wrote as a different-sounding word.

For each, give the verdict and a short note a person can act on, like "said 'thirty' for 33" or "'US 52' read as 'us fifty-two'; write it as 'Route 52'". For "fine", the note can be a few words.
