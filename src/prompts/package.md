Write the packaging for the video "{{title}}" on Everyday Case Study, a YouTube channel of case studies on how the world really works, from a small-town store closing to a company in the news: the title and thumbnail a viewer sees before clicking, and the description under the video. The channel is modeled on Modern MBA, whose titles state an argument and whose thumbnails put a few big words over a real picture.

## Titles

Write five, best first. Each states what the video argues or reveals, naming the concrete thing (the store, the town, the product), in words a curious person would click: "Nobody Makes Money Renting Cars", not "The Economics of Car Rental". At most 70 characters, ideally under 60. Promise only what the video delivers: no number it doesn't say, no "shocking" or "insane", no all caps, no question it leaves unanswered.

## Thumbnails

Write three. Each is two to five words on one to three short lines that add to a title rather than repeat it: the stakes, the number, the turn. Pick the one word to color, the one that carries the punch. A number or claim must be exactly as strong as the narration makes it: where it gives a range or an estimate, use the range or its low end ("20%+", not "30%", when it says "an estimated 20 to 30 percent"), and don't state as fact what it says sources disagree on. Choose a background from the numbered images below, which come from the video: a real photo of a place in the story, or an aerial view, where the site in the middle gets circled in pen. The picture should show what the words are about. Put the words on the side that's darker or emptier, away from the picture's subject, say which side, and for a photo say where its subject is.

## Description

Two or three short paragraphs that retell the story: the places and companies by name, what happened, and why it matters to the viewer. Plain sentences. No hashtags, no emoji, no "in this video", and don't give away the ending's best line.

Then a chapter title for every part of the script below, two to six words, the way a viewer scanning the chapters would want them; the hook and setup can share one opening chapter.

## Tags

Up to twelve searches people would type to find this video: the places, companies and topics.
