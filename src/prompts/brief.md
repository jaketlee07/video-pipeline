Use web search to build a research brief. Jake should be able to fact-check it in about an hour, and a script will be written from it. Look for the surprising, concrete material that makes the video worth watching: real numbers, named places and companies, dated events, studies and what they found, and the mechanism that connects them. Find at least one specific real case the video can follow, such as a named town, store or company. Find the history too: how things got this way, in dated steps, and the people, companies and funds whose decisions drove it. Also find the non-obvious conclusion the evidence actually supports, which becomes the video's twist.

The video puts its evidence on screen, so look for primary documents worth showing: court records, company filings and announcements, deeds, laws, studies and local news reports, and the exact words in them that matter.

Write the brief in markdown inside <brief> tags. Start with this line, before any heading:

**Angle:** one or two sentences giving the video's thesis and why it's surprising.

Then these sections, each with a ### heading:

### Key facts
A bulleted list. Each bullet is one specific claim with its number, date and place, followed by its source as a markdown link. Mark anything uncertain, outdated or disputed with ⚠️ and say why.

### Real cases
Two to four specific cases (a town, a company or an event): what happened, when, and the numbers, with linked sources.

### Timeline
The story in dated steps, oldest first: what happened, who decided it, and the number that changed, each with its source link. This becomes the video's chapters.

### How it works
The economic mechanism in plain language: who pays, who gains, and why it plays out this way. End with **Money flows**: one bullet per payment or transfer, written as "Who → To whom: what for (amount, source)", with amounts only where a source gives them. Include flows that stop or get blocked (a closed store's sales tax, a deed that bars a grocer). These become the video's animated diagrams.

### Quotes and documents
Four to eight short excerpts that could be shown on screen, from court records, filings, company statements, deeds, laws, studies or news reports. For each: the exact words as they appear in the source (under 40 words, copied from the search result, never reworded or recalled from memory), who said or wrote them, the type of document, the date and the link. If you can't see a source's exact wording, leave it out.

### Outline
Three hook ideas of one sentence each, each dropping into one real moment (a named place on a date). Then the chapters in story order, each with the turn it covers, the named people or companies in it, and the fact, case or quote that backs its reveal. Note where the money flows get explained. Then the twist or takeaway, and a related video to tease at the end.

### Chart and map ideas
Datasets from the facts above that would make good animated charts, with the actual values and the source. Also list the real places worth showing on a map or in an aerial shot.

### Fact-check list
The five to eight numbers the script will rely on most, each with its source link, so Jake can verify those first. The quotes above appear on screen word for word, so list them here too.

### Sources
Every source used, as a numbered list of markdown links with the publisher and year.
