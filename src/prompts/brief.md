Sub-format: {{subFormat}}

Use web search to build a research brief. Jake should be able to fact-check it in about an hour, and a script will be written from it. Look for the surprising, concrete material that makes the video worth watching: real numbers, named places and companies, dated events, studies and what they found, and the mechanism that connects them. Find at least one specific real case the video can follow, such as a named town, store or company. Also find the non-obvious conclusion the evidence actually supports, which becomes the video's twist.

Write the brief in markdown inside <brief> tags. Start with this line, before any heading:

**Angle:** one or two sentences giving the video's thesis and why it's surprising.

Then these sections, each with a ### heading:

### Key facts
A bulleted list. Each bullet is one specific claim with its number, date and place, followed by its source as a markdown link. Mark anything uncertain, outdated or disputed with ⚠️ and say why.

### Real cases
Two to four specific cases (a town, a company or an event): what happened, when, and the numbers, with linked sources.

### How it works
The economic mechanism in plain language: who pays, who gains, and why it plays out this way.

### Outline
Three hook ideas of one sentence each. Then three to five sections, each with its single reveal and the fact or case that backs it. Then the twist or takeaway, and a related video to tease at the end.

### Chart and map ideas
Datasets from the facts above that would make good animated charts, with the actual values and the source. Also list the real places worth showing on a map or in an aerial shot.

### Fact-check list
The five to eight numbers the script will rely on most, each with its source link, so Jake can verify those first.

### Sources
Every source used, as a numbered list of markdown links with the publisher and year.
