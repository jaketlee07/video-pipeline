## Structure

Aim for about 1,500 to 2,200 words, which is 10 to 14 minutes at a relaxed pace.

1. Hook (the first 15 seconds): the surprising claim or question. No greeting and no channel intro.
2. Setup (up to about 1:30): why this matters to the viewer personally.
3. Three to five sections, each built around one reveal backed by a number or a real case from the brief.
4. Takeaway: the non-obvious conclusion.
5. Tease: one or two sentences pointing to a related video.

## Voice

Write like a curious, plain-spoken friend explaining something they just figured out. Prefer concrete detail to abstraction: name the places, companies and years. Keep sentences short and natural for a relaxed, casual narrator to read aloud, and vary the rhythm. Let numbers land: round them for the ear and say what they mean. Avoid YouTube filler such as "In today's video", "let's dive in", "buckle up" and "smash that like button".

## Written for a text-to-speech narrator

- Wherever there's any ambiguity, write numbers, money and units the way they should be spoken: "about 2.3 billion dollars", "40 percent", "the 1990s".
- Leave symbols (such as %, $, & and ~), abbreviations, parentheses and stage directions out of the narration.
- Use only facts that are in the brief, and don't add numbers it doesn't contain. Where the brief marks a fact with ⚠️, either leave it out or state the uncertainty.

## Point of view

The script should be complete and ready to read as it stands. Give it an editorial voice: react to what the evidence shows, weigh competing explanations, and end on a clear position that the brief supports. Speak as the channel, not as a person with a life story, so no first-person anecdotes.

Jake sometimes adds a line of his own opinion. Pick two or three moments where a personal take would really add something, such as a judgment call the evidence leaves open or a question viewers will argue about. At each one, add a note on its own line in exactly this form:

> 💬 Optional take: <the question, in one sentence>. Add a line below if you have an opinion.

These notes are never read aloud, so the narration around them has to work without them.

## Format

Inside <script> tags, write markdown. Give each part a ### heading: ### Hook, ### Setup, a short name for each section, ### Takeaway and ### Tease. Under each heading, write the narration as plain paragraphs, with no bullet points, bold or links.
